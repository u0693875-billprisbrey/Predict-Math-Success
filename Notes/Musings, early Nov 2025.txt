SUMMARY:

- Historically, roughly ACT Math test would determine the course and HSGPA would determine the grade.
  - I am thinking of this as math ability and study ability.
- A few things have changed:

  (1) Mix of courses has changed.	
        1010 is all-but abandoned; replaced by 1090

  (2) Fewer test score submissions
  	- Less than half of students are submitting ACT test scores
  	- This is an increasingly elite group of students:  higher HSGPA and math grades than non-submitters; higher ACT median than historically
	- This self-selection will make it harder to generalize from the test-submitting population to the general population	


  (3) Reduced course selection accuracy:
	- The connection between "prior" information and course selection is approaching random or no relationship		
	- This is not taking into account knowledge of who submitted and did not submit test scores (this would improve the model)
	

  (3) Improved grade prediction	
	- R-squared for grade prediction has improved 
	- I'm not sure why.  Right now I suspect it is because a larger proportion of test submitters get A's as they are both better at math and better students.
        	 
  (4) Course selection doesn't seem to matter very much		
	- I'm not sure why.  Right now I suspect it is because test submitters are better students, making course choice more irrelevant 
	- This is the area of next focus		


 
- Possible next steps include:
	- Predict course and grade for the non-submitters
	- Include a submission flag prediction variable, and impute test scores	(probably knn)
	- Include SAT scores (why not?)	
- Develop "warning" threshold:  Can I identify at-risk students?	
- Focus on predicting W's.
