  DISCUSSION:   
    The predictive models could predict grade accuracy with r-squared values between [[0.2 and 0.28]].  Consistently the leading predictors were the ACT math test scores and the high school GPA.  Adopting a model of performance [[cite!]] where performance is the outcome of ability by motivation, the models seem to suggest that "ability" can be broken into "math ability" (indicated by the ACT math test scores) and "study ability" (indicated by the high school GPA.)  No metrics related to "motivation" were used. 

    Derivatives were calculated from the two leading predictors:  z-scores (based on successful students in the prior year) and a combined z-score distance.  These derived variables were found to improve predictive accuracy.

    Predictive accuracy may be improved with the addition of concurrent data, such as the number of credits concurrently taken, the difficulty of concurrent courses, and early grades on homework, quizzes, and tests.	 	

  Q1 CONCLUSIONS: 

    Grade performance can be predicted at an R-squared value of 0.2 to 0.28 with minimal pre-University data.	
    The most important predictors are high school GPA, ACT math test scores, and their derivatives.   
    This model can be used to differentiate "high risk" and "low risk" groups of students (where one out of three students fail or one out of twenty-five students fail.)  This designation may direct interventions.

    DISCUSSION:

    The time series analysis demonstrated that minimal pre-University data could be used to predict course selection until 2022.  It also validated the use of the combined z-score distance as an important predictor.	

    The ability to predict courses declined over time, and all-but vanished in 2023 and 2024.  A closer look at the combined distance variable shows a discontinuity in this metric starting in 2020 and accelerating in 2023-2024.  This collapse of the model accuracy and accelerating discontinuity may suggest an urgent need to address math course placement.  On the other hand, over the same period grades improved (or inflated or compressed), suggeting changing expectations for math performance in these initial math courses. 
	
    Q2 CONCLUSIONS: 

	Math course recommendations can be made by comparing incoming student qualifications to the qualifications of the prior year's successful students.	

	This should be considered a loose guideline with a heavy overlap in acceptable courses per student.
